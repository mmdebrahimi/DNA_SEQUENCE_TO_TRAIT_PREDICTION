## P4 — the pre-registered discrimination

The bands were frozen in advance and are disjoint: **H-organism 60.4–80.4 %**, **H-substrate 81.1–100 %**.

> Measured P2 = **95.5 %** → **H-substrate SELECTED**; H-organism and H-neither **rejected**.

The completed 2×2:

| P2 flat-deficit | carbon | nitrogen |
|---|---|---|
| **E. coli** (`iML1515`) | 76.9 % *(833/1083)* | 91.1 % |
| **P. putida** (`iJN1463`) | 70.4 % | **95.5 %** |

- **Between-axis gap: +19.6 pp** (carbon mean 73.7 %, nitrogen mean 93.3 %).
- **Within-axis organism spread: 6.5 pp** (carbon) and **4.5 pp** (nitrogen).

So the substrate axis moves P2 roughly **3–4× more** than the organism does.

**The strong form is that the direction replicates *within* each organism independently** — nitrogen >
carbon in E. coli (91.1 > 76.9) *and* in P. putida (95.5 > 70.4). That is what makes this more than a
cross-cell comparison: each organism is its own controlled contrast, so the ordering does not depend on
comparing `iML1515` against `iJN1463`.

## What it means

The conditional-essentiality deficit — already established as a **model** problem (most missed essential
cells are deletions that changed nothing) — is **worse on nitrogen panels than carbon panels, in both
organisms**. On a nitrogen panel, ~19 of every 20 missed essential genes are deletions the model says do
nothing at all.

Practically: **carbon panels are where FBA conditional essentiality has the most headroom.** Nitrogen
panels are closer to the model's ceiling, so effort spent improving predictions there has less to work
with.

## The obvious mechanism was tested and is NOT supported

The natural explanation is that nitrogen conditions perturb the network *less* — they all share glucose
as the carbon source, so the metabolic state barely moves between conditions and deletions land flat.

**The available proxy does not support that.** Coefficient of variation of wildtype growth across
conditions:

| panel | wt CV |
|---|---|
| E. coli nitrogen | 0.308 |
| P. putida carbon | 0.316 |
| P. putida nitrogen | 0.334 |

Essentially identical — the nitrogen panels do **not** show a narrower spread of metabolic states by this
measure. So the mechanism is **unexplained**, and is deliberately not published as fact here. (The E. coli
carbon artifact predates the `wildtype_growth` field, so that cell has no CV; three panels is enough to
see there is no separation.)

## Honest limits

1. **n = 1 per cell.** P4 discriminates between two pre-named hypotheses on a single new measurement. It
   promotes H-substrate to leading candidate; it does not establish it. The within-organism replication of
   the *direction* is what carries most of the weight, not the exact percentages.
2. **Organism and model quality are NOT separable** by this design. "P. putida" and "`iJN1463` is less
   curated than `iML1515`" are the same column. The conclusion is stated as substrate-axis-dominant, which
   is unaffected — but no organism-level claim is made.
3. **Panel sizes differ** (25 / 13 / 13 / 27). `H-neither` explicitly covered "P2 tracks panel size" and
   was rejected, but note the two nitrogen panels are the *smallest and largest*, which is mild evidence
   against a size effect rather than a test of one.
4. **P3's lift is the weakest of the four here** (+0.0330 vs +0.1444, +0.1008). The model still beats the
   constant null on this panel, but not by much.

## Method note — mapping was widened without loosening it

The panel needed more than the 9 conditions the curated E. coli map covers. The 18 additional conditions
were admitted by **exact metabolite-name equality** against the model's own names after stripping
salt/hydrate forms (`Adenine hydrochloride hydrate` → `EX_ade_e`, metabolite named `adenine`), never by
substring or fuzzy match — the discipline earned when a hand-guessed mapping in the stress probe paired
`sodium fluoride` with ferrous iron. `test_a_substring_near_miss_is_REFUSED` pins it: `Adenosine` must not
bind to `adenine`. The Keio path keeps the original curated-only builder so the shipped E. coli result
stays byte-reproducible.
