## Highest-confidence rows (top 5)

1. **LegNet §3.3 — "The primary architectural changes did not affect the model performance significantly" + "the training mode was more important than the minor features of the model architecture", with a receptive field deliberately capped at 79 bp on a 150 bp input.** This is the single most directly responsive finding: the best-in-class short-sequence promoter model found architecture detail near-irrelevant and did not pursue extra span.
2. **Urtecho et al. Fig 7 — CNN R2 = 0.31 regression / AUPRC 0.44 classification on 150 bp E. coli promoter tiles, with logistic regression at 0.53 beating the CNN, and all other families inside 0.26–0.33.** Published precedent on the same substrate family, showing deep models failing to separate from simple ones.
3. **ENCODE Nature 2025 — MPRALegNet Pearson r = 0.83 "comparable to the technical noise of the assay itself", yet "log-linear improvement in performance as a function of training set size".** Supplies both the ceiling framing and the one data-scale lever with a measured functional form.
4. **Gupta & Rush §3 Dataset 2 — "accessible regions locally have highly explanatory motifs and having additional context far away does not improve the accuracy", with Table 2 showing no improvement on DNase.** The mechanism for when span buys nothing, from the paper that introduced dilated convolutions to genomics.
5. **Frontiers in Genetics 2019 — a Random Forest on irrelevant features scored 0.75 AUCROC on the CAGI saturation-mutagenesis split, and "the performance of the holdout models drastically degraded for all reporters".** The decisive caution against reading a fixed-background design as evidence of sequence understanding.

## Honest gaps

1. **No paper was found that runs the specific ablation the question asks for: a clean sweep of receptive-field span on a FIXED ~150 bp regulatory element, reporting no gain.** The closest evidence is indirect and three-part: (a) LegNet explicitly capping its receptive field at 79 bp on a 150 bp input because that is "close to the actual variable length of the tested promoter", and reporting that architectural changes did not matter; (b) Gupta & Rush's mechanism statement that locally-motif-explained signals do not benefit from distant context; (c) the DREAM consortium's claim that "attention may not be the most efficient way of capturing cis-regulatory logic in short sequences". A direct span-sweep null result on short elements appears to be **unpublished** as far as this search reached. State this as an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

2. **No paper was found that frames a plateau specifically against a GC-content-only baseline.** The literature consistently frames plateaus against *replicate reproducibility* instead (MPRA-DragoNN 0.28 vs 0.40; MPRALegNet 0.83 vs technical noise). The nearest GC-relevant findings are descriptive rather than baseline comparisons — e.g. a neuronal MPRA reporting repressor tiles at 74% median GC vs 45% for the rest of the library, and the NAR 2025 bacterial paper's observation that "GC content might have less influence on promoter strength than AT content". **A model-vs-GC-baseline comparison as a reported control appears to be rare or absent in this literature**, which means the caller's own GC baseline is a stronger control than the field typically runs, and there is no published number to compare 0.3112 against.

3. **EvoAug's per-augmentation effect sizes are figure-only.** The fetch returned `VALUE NOT PRESENT` for numeric Pearson/Spearman gains on DeepSTARR; the paper states directions ("improved for most ... with the exception of reverse-complement and random mutations") but the magnitudes live in Fig 2a and Supplementary Fig 5 images. Any effect size for augmentation on a 249 nt task would need the supplementary tables or the EvoAug repo.

4. **Lagator et al.'s R2 values are figure-only.** The fetch returned `VALUE NOT PRESENT` for numeric variance-explained figures; Fig 2C reports "fraction of variance explained on evaluation data" and the raw r2 values sit in Table S3, which was not retrieved. So the fixed-background-vs-random-sequence contrast is captured only as the authors' directional claim, not as a number.

5. **No bacterial promoter MPRA result was found that is reported as a fraction of its own replicate ceiling.** Urtecho reports both a ceiling (r = 0.965) and a model score (R2 = 0.31) but never divides them; the "fraction of ceiling" framing exists only for human MPRA (MPRA-DragoNN's 61%, and MPRALegNet's qualitative "comparable to technical noise"). **For bacteria specifically, the ceiling-normalised state of the art appears not to have been published.**

6. **Vaishnav et al. 2022 (Nature) was never fetched directly.** Its numbers reach this memo only through third-party comparisons (Camformer's abstract: reference model r2 = 0.879, ρ = 0.938, 20th place in DREAM 2022). Any claim about what the Vaishnav work itself says about transfer to other organisms or to bacteria is therefore **unsupported by this run**.

7. **Nothing was found on whether yeast GPRA models transfer to bacterial promoters.** Sub-question 3 asked specifically whether Vaishnav/Kinney/de Boer-style work transfers. No cross-kingdom transfer experiment surfaced in this search. The two systems differ in data scale by 2–3 orders of magnitude (10^6–10^7 yeast sequences vs 10^4–10^5 bacterial), which is itself the most likely obstruction, but that is **inference, not a measured finding**.

8. **Three publisher paywalls / auth walls blocked fetches**: Nature Communications s41467-022-32829-5 (auth redirect, twice), Genome Biology via both BMC and SpringerLink (auth redirect, twice — EvoAug was recovered via its bioRxiv preprint instead), Genes & Development review (HTTP 403), and PMC articles PMC6834773 / PMC10716054 / PMC9433444 (cookie wall). Where a preprint or alternative route existed it was used and is cited; where none existed the row is marked `low` confidence with `VALUE NOT PRESENT`.

9. **The caller's substrate figures were taken as given and NOT verified**, per the Intent Contract's out-of-scope boundary: n = 44,106; 150 bp tiles; replicate ceiling Spearman 0.941; model scores 0.3234 / 0.3112 / 0.3167. Note one observation that follows from the sourced rows rather than from any verification: the Urtecho paper reports its own bacterial replicate reproducibility as r = 0.965 on the 150 bp TSS library, which is in the same neighbourhood as 0.941 — but the two are different quantities measured on different libraries and should not be equated.
